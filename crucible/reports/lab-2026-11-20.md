### Furnace build: three new Gifts and a fourth feat

The review asked for more identity for the Living Furnace. Two ideas were measured.

Dropped: "terrain you set". Gifts that paid while your own Field Spell was the terrain fired too rarely to matter (0.6 times a duel) and the Furnace build got worse with them, 36.8 to 34.7. FIRE as a condition is also thin: 55 of 781 monsters are FIRE, and the old Kindling fires in one duel out of twenty unless the kit is Fire and Scale.

Built: one melded monster that grows while it stays alive. Each of the other builds pays the next meld; these pay the monster already on the table.

- Banked Coals: at the start of your turn your strongest face-up melded monster gets +300 ATK, up to four times a duel. Alone from duel one: 9.4 against 4.9.
- Quench: when your melded monster destroys a rival monster in battle it gets +200 ATK and DEF, up to four times a duel. Alone: 9.3 against 4.9.
- Cinder Fall: at the end of your turn, if you hold a face-up monster with 2500 ATK or more, the rival loses 400 Life Points, up to four times a duel. Alone it does nothing for the CPU (4.5), as with every Life Point clock so far.
- Feat, Master Smith: win 8 duels with a monster of 3,000 ATK or more on your side. Unlocks Cinder Fall. The CPU scores about 5 a run.

Furnace build, no Forger, Dragons and Warriors kit, 1,500 runs: 36.8 before, 41.2 with the three Gifts in the pool. The same kit with no build preference clears 43.3, so Furnace is still about two points behind taking the best Gift on offer. With Tarn on that kit Furnace is 65.7 against 63.4. The other builds did not move with the pool at 21 Gifts: neutral 46.8, Ambush 45.7, Tomb 47.6, Storm 68.1.

Not assessed: no person has played these. Whether guarding one growing monster feels different from the other builds is a question for hands, not for the CPU.

The cause. A cross meld gives a monster of one parent's type and the other's attribute, the next one above the stronger parent. So how far a kit climbs depends on which monsters exist for its own types and attributes. Some kits reach about 1,850 ATK from an opening hand with three or four cards, others about 1,530. That 300 ATK is worth roughly 25 points of clear rate. The size of the ladders does not predict it (the richest ladders, Grave Court and Blades and Claws, climb in small steps and are weak). The Tide has a second problem: its WATER monsters lose attribute-wheel fights to EARTH, the most common attribute among rivals, which costs it about 12 more points.

The switch. `KIT_TUNE` in `engine/run.mjs` changes one number per kit: the strongest monster the kit may hold (1,500 for every kit before). Weak-climbing kits get stronger starting monsters, strong-climbing kits get weaker ones, and the kit screen shows the average ATK, so the trade is visible. With no Forger the kits now clear 39.7 to 49.5 (before: 14.5 to 62.3). The Tide is still lowest. Adding a fourth type to The Tide was tried and did not help (23.6).

With their Forgers, even kits on:

|---|---|---|

| Scrapper | 75.5 / 60.1 / 51.9 | 62.3 / 58.9 / 64.6 |

### Why Storm is ahead, what every item is worth alone, and a "tuned items" switch (`itemTune: 1`)

All numbers here are the expert CPU on the page's default switches (five Gifts, leaning offers, Heat rules, even kits), no Forger. Nothing in this section was played by a person.

**The cause.** With every Storm piece locked out of the offers the Storm player clears 47.3%, the same as everyone else. Locking pieces one at a time: without Copper Loop 58.3%, without Patient Coil 61.4%, with both 68.0%. Two items carry the build. It is not the Storm play style: a neutral player who only prefers Storm Gifts (`pick_storm`) clears 64.1%.

|---|---|---|
| Storm (plays long melds, prefers Storm) | 68.0 | 54.8 |

| Tomb | 48.6 | 40.8 |

**One item alone** (1,500 runs each, the item held from duel one, no other Gifts; with no Gifts the CPU clears 22.5%). Points gained: Copper Loop +27.7, Grave Ward +26.0, Patient Coil +22.8, Tinder Vow +13.6, Snare +13.2, Banked Coals +12.5, Whetstone +11.8, Home Ground +11.0, Grounding Rod +10.2, Deep Pockets +9.8, Quench +9.7, Tomb Ledger +8.8, Static Field +8.4, Deep Shade +6.3, Overcharge +5.4, Ossuary +5.0, Spite +4.5, Living Furnace +4.2, Deep Grave +3.6, Ashen Echo +3.4, False Floor +3.0, Cinder Fall +2.2. About 25 items sit within a point of zero for the CPU (every item that only costs the rival Life Points is among them: the CPU wins on the board, not by burn). Broken Anvil costs 10.2 points.

Three items are each worth more than twice the fourth. What they share: attack points on the first meld of a duel, or a free removal of the monster that just beat yours. Tempo decides these duels.

**Variants measured alone** (points over no Gifts): Copper Loop once a duel +300 / +300: +18.2; twice at +200 ATK: +13.8; once at +200 / +200: +6.8; twice at +300 / +300 but asking four cards: +11.3. Patient Coil +200 / +200: +14.0; +300 ATK only: +21.8; +200 ATK: +13.8; asking five cards: -3.0 (the CPU overreaches for it). Grave Ward once a duel: +14.6; once at four graveyard monsters: +13.4; twice at five: +19.4.

**The switch.** `itemTune: 1`: every Storm piece asks for a meld of four or more cards, not three; Copper Loop gives +200 ATK (twice a duel); Patient Coil +200 ATK and DEF; Grave Ward once a duel. The two rival styles that carry those pieces (Meldwright, Stormhand) follow the same rules and say so on the door. The gap from Storm to the next build falls from 19 points to 11. With rival styles on the neutral player clears 36.8% (41.5% without the tuning).

**Still open.**
- Storm is still the best build by 11 points.
- The whole game got about six points harder (neutral 46.6% to 40.2%) and nothing was given back.
- Ambush, Furnace and Lean pieces are worth less to the CPU than taking no build at all with neutral play. Lifting the weak builds is the better next move than cutting Storm further, and it should wait until a person has played, since most of their pieces pay in Life Points and choices the CPU does not value.
- Tinder Vow (+13.6) has no real cost for a deck that melds every turn. Broken Anvil is a trap pick.

### More to earn after run nine: the "Long road" switch (off by default)

Today a profile runs out of new things quickly. The expert CPU, buying whatever it can afford after each run, empties the Vault by run 4 and has every feat by run 3; after that the only new thing is the next Heat level, and by run 30 most profiles have cleared Heat 10. The Long road adds four things, all variety and none a stronger start (`engine/meta.mjs`, every function takes `long`):

- **Starter kits.** A Forger starts with their first kit, dealt three ways. Their second kit opens at 10 duels won as that Forger, their third at 30 (`KIT_GOALS`). Lost runs count.
- **Heat records per Forger.** Heat stays shared, as the design map says: clearing a level opens the next one for every Forger. Each Forger keeps their own best Heat cleared (`byForger[f].heat`), shown on the Forger screen and announced at the end of a run as a new record. (The first build of this switch, playable Version 20, gave each Forger their own Heat ladder. That went against the design map and was changed in Version 21.)
- **Enchants.** Six are open from the start, six are bought in the Vault at 50 Memories each. An Anvil offers up to three of those held; the shuffle uses the dice exactly as before.
- **Three more feats** (Stone Wall, Kept Secret, Last Light) for the three Ambush Gifts that were built but never offered (Shield Oath, Cracked Mask, Cold Lantern).

A profile keeps the per-Forger record (`byForger`: runs, duels won, clears, best Heat) whether or not the switch is on, so turning it on later counts earlier play.
