## Overnight 19 to 20 Nov: the first progression loop, offers, free Trap, rival styles

All numbers: 3,000 expert-CPU runs, seeds from 1, five-Gift preset, no Forger unless named. Simulator only. Nothing here was judged by a person.

### What was built

- Feats (`engine/meta.mjs`): Storm Hand (12 melds of four or more cards) unlocks Grounding Rod; Grave Tender (8 duels won with eight or more of your monsters in the graveyard) unlocks Grave Ward; Patient Trap (rivals attack into your veiled monsters or Traps 12 times) unlocks Snare. Progress is banked win or lose. Per run the expert CPU makes 13 four-card melds, wins 6 duels with a full graveyard and is attacked into 8 times (15 on the shadow brain), so it finishes the first feat in run one and the others by run two. A person will be slower; not measured.
- A newly unlocked Gift is shown once, marked NEW, in the first Gift offer of the next run (`newRun` option `featured`).
- Codex: 530 result pages (490 cross melds, 39 two-card printed fusions, 1 three-card printed fusion; counted by trying every pair of the 781 monsters). A found page states the rule that made it and the ATK range that makes it again. An unfound page shows only Type and Attribute.
- Page: run-end screen with feat bars and the unlock, Feats screen, one Gift re-deal a run, "fits your pieces" tags.

### Furnace build: three new Gifts and a fourth feat

The review asked for more identity for the Living Furnace. Two ideas were measured.

Dropped: "terrain you set". Gifts that paid while your own Field Spell was the terrain fired too rarely to matter (0.6 times a duel) and the Furnace build got worse with them, 36.8 to 34.7. FIRE as a condition is also thin: 55 of 781 monsters are FIRE, and the old Kindling fires in one duel out of twenty unless the kit is Fire and Scale.

Built: one melded monster that grows while it stays alive. Each of the other builds pays the next meld; these pay the monster already on the table.

- Banked Coals: at the start of your turn your strongest face-up melded monster gets +300 ATK, up to four times a duel. Alone from duel one: 9.4 against 4.9.
- Quench: when your melded monster destroys a rival monster in battle it gets +200 ATK and DEF, up to four times a duel. Alone: 9.3 against 4.9.
- Cinder Fall: at the end of your turn, if you hold a face-up monster with 2500 ATK or more, the rival loses 400 Life Points, up to four times a duel. Alone it does nothing for the CPU (4.5), as with every Life Point clock so far.
- Feat, Master Smith: win 8 duels with a monster of 3,000 ATK or more on your side. Unlocks Cinder Fall. The CPU scores about 5 a run.

Furnace build, no Forger, Dragons and Warriors kit, 1,500 runs: 36.8 before, 41.2 with the three Gifts in the pool. The same kit with no build preference clears 43.3, so Furnace is still about two points behind taking the best Gift on offer. With Tarn on that kit Furnace is 65.7 against 63.4. The other builds did not move with the pool at 21 Gifts: neutral 46.8, Ambush 45.7, Tomb 47.6, Storm 68.1.

Not assessed: no person has played these. Whether guarding one growing monster feels different from the other builds is a question for hands, not for the CPU.

### Why kits are unequal, and an "even kits" switch (`kitTune: 1`)

Forced kit, no Forger, so the Forger's own rule is out of the picture (lever `testKit`, 1,000 to 1,500 runs each):

| Kit | Clear before | Best ATK from an opening hand, up to four cards | Cap on its strongest monster, even kits | Clear, even kits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fire and Scale | 62.3 | 1753 | 1200 | 46.6 |
| Stone and Gears | 57.4 | 1799 | 1425 | 47.1 |
| Lantern Choir | 56.7 | 1683 | 1350 | 48.6 |
| High Wind | 50.8 | 1861 | 1400 | 42.5 |
| Spellcasters and Fiends | 48.1 | 1612 | 1500 (unchanged) | 48.5 |
| Machines and Thunder | 45.0 | 1719 | 1500 (unchanged) | 45.9 |
| Beasts and Plants | 41.4 | 1636 | 1650 | 46.1 |
| Dragons and Warriors | 41.0 | 1660 | 1600 | 43.7 |
| The Swarm | 38.0 | 1528 | 1700 | 49.1 |
| Grave Court | 33.7 | 1586 | 1800 | 47.3 |
| Blades and Claws | 30.1 | 1540 | 1800 | 49.5 |
| The Tide | 14.5 | 1549 | 2000, kit weight 118 | 39.7 |

The cause. A cross meld gives a monster of one parent's type and the other's attribute, the next one above the stronger parent. So how far a kit climbs depends on which monsters exist for its own types and attributes. Some kits reach about 1,850 ATK from an opening hand with three or four cards, others about 1,530. That 300 ATK is worth roughly 25 points of clear rate. The size of the ladders does not predict it (the richest ladders, Grave Court and Blades and Claws, climb in small steps and are weak). The Tide has a second problem: its WATER monsters lose attribute-wheel fights to EARTH, the most common attribute among rivals, which costs it about 12 more points.

The switch. `KIT_TUNE` in `engine/run.mjs` changes one number per kit: the strongest monster the kit may hold (1,500 for every kit before). Weak-climbing kits get stronger starting monsters, strong-climbing kits get weaker ones, and the kit screen shows the average ATK, so the trade is visible. With no Forger the kits now clear 39.7 to 49.5 (before: 14.5 to 62.3). The Tide is still lowest. Adding a fourth type to The Tide was tried and did not help (23.6).

With their Forgers, even kits on:

| Forger | Kits before | Kits now |
|---|---|---|
| Tarn | 76.9 / 63.4 / 52.1 | 66.9 / 64.9 / 71.0 |
| Scrapper | 75.5 / 60.1 / 51.9 | 62.3 / 58.9 / 64.6 |
| Veilwalker | 56.7 / 46.9 / 34.9 | 48.2 / 46.9 / 47.0 |
| Stormcaller | 72.0 / 58.9 / 36.0 | 63.1 / 61.4 / 65.3 |

What this shows about Forgers. With kits evened out, Tarn, the Scrapper and the Stormcaller are each worth 15 to 20 points over no Forger (about 47). The Veilwalker is worth nothing: her three kits clear the same with her as without her. She is the one Forger whose rule needs a redesign, which the review also asked for.

In the playable this is the Lab switch "Even kits", off by default. The kit screen's lab note shows the test CPU's clear rate for whichever setting is on. Not assessed: no person has played either setting, and whether a kit of weaker cards that melds well feels fair next to a kit of stronger cards is a question for hands.
