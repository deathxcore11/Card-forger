3.85.14 - Power with explicit evidence and build goals

Power evidence chips wrap on narrow screens instead of overflowing.

3.55.0 — Pressure-tested Deck Power and Matchups, faster builders
Deck Power was run against real winning lists in all three games, the same lists deliberately
weakened, and decks built to cheat. What changed:
- A close copy of a tournament deck is judged on the list, not just the archetype's results. Copies
  short of what the archetype's lists usually play cost in proportion (staples too: Boss's Orders,
  hand traps, Sol Ring), cards no placing list plays cost, and the score can't sit far above how
  well the list itself is built. Trimming a copy of each key card now costs about 8–20; cutting a

- Card quality matters: a pile no placing list resembles is judged by that, not by a tidy curve.
- Yu-Gi-Oh!: when the player going first bricks, the other deck still has to be able to do something.
- Pokémon: spread damage (Dragapult ex) softens the next target and Knocks Out benched Pokémon.

- Magic deck builder shows its versions without waiting on the cheapest-printing check (it runs

- Pokémon: the backup price source (often down) is skipped after two failures, the cheapest-printing

3.45.0 — Deck Power that can't be fooled, in each game's own terms
- Deck Power was tested against decks built to trick it, and against real tournament winners in
  all three games. The fakes used to score 60–88; now each is held down by the flaw a player would
  spot. Real top decks (Standard, Pioneer, Modern, Pokémon Standard, Yu-Gi-Oh! Advanced) score 74–100.
- New "What holds this deck back" box on the Power tab: deal-breakers that cap the score until
  they're fixed, each explained. The scouting verdict names the biggest one first.
- Magic, built on Gavin Verhey's deck-building rule (a deck with a mission, not a pile):
  - Plan check: names cards that work against the deck's plan, like life gain or board wipes in
    aggro, or combat tricks in control. Life gain counts as on-plan when the deck pays it off, and
    cards with cost reducers, Warp or cycling aren't called too slow.
  - Critical mass: tribal, spells-matter and artifact decks need enough pieces for their payoffs
    (about 18 of the tribe, 20 noncreature spells, 16 artifacts). Below that the score is capped.
  - Midrange with no theme is judged on cards that out-value the opponent.
  - Can't cast its spells (no source of a main color), no way to win, and creatures far below a
    fair rate for their cost are deal-breakers. Hybrid mana no longer demands both colors, mana
    rocks count as sources, splash colors need fewer sources, and landfall decks may run extra lands.
- Yu-Gi-Oh!: searchers only count as starters when the deck runs what they search for (named
  cards, Field Spells, the right Type and Attribute). Extra Deck cards the Main Deck can't summon
  (Xyz with no monsters of that Level, Synchro with no Tuner, Fusion materials it doesn't run) are
  named and don't count. Hand traps and staples with no engine are capped.
- Pokémon: damage is judged as it plays: scaling attacks, damage counters (Alakazam), attacks
  that hit any Pokémon, Abilities that attack twice, and attacks copied from other Pokémon
  (N's Zoroark ex). Decks that share the attacking between several Pokémon, recover attackers or
  swarm with single-Prize Pokémon get credit for it. A best attacker whose Energy the deck doesn't
  run, or a main line too thin to survive one Prize, is a deal-breaker.
- Tournament matching ignores the generic staples every deck plays, so a list only counts as a
  copy of a winning deck when it shares that deck's own cards.
- Fixed: Pokémon Deck Power could crash on a list whose cards hadn't finished loading.
