## Use the available space deliberately

Give the battlefield the main share of the desktop window. Anchor compact
player/zone information and phase controls at the edges. Use a purposeful right
rail for inspection/log/stack context that can give room back to the table;
closing it must not hide an unanswered decision. Let the bottom hand use the
available width without cropping away names, selected cards or play controls.
Card lift should not jump the whole layout. Test density as the board fills,
not only on an almost-empty opening table. Do not fill quiet table space with
unrelated panels or enlarge every control to consume it.

Picking up a card asks the authority for current legal choices. Highlight the
actual permitted play area, destination and targets; the ghost/drop preview
tracks the pointer without implying a committed move. Lands, permanents,
attachments and stack spells retain their different rules. A legal drop opens
any required target/payment/choice preview, then commits through the same path
as an explicit Play action. An invalid or stale drop restores the uncommitted
card and supplies a concise reason. Inspection remains separate, and click/
keyboard alternatives preserve the same choices. Highlighting must never make
an illegal action legal or spend resources before acceptance.

## A table that feels made

Start with one finished warm, textured table: fine material grain, restrained
etched detail near the edges, soft vignette and directional light. Keep the
central card area quiet. Texture must remain subtle at native scale and high
DPI; it must not lower the contrast of cards, selection outlines or numbers.
Avoid busy repeating patterns, constant ambient motion and giant empty panels.

Separate lands, permanents, stack, hand, graveyard/exile/library and command
zones where relevant. Zone occupancy, stack growth and a crowded hand must
shape the layout rather than overlap controls. Preserve enough card size for
identity and effective stats; larger inspection is always explicitly reachable.
Normal collection navigation returns after the match.

Use representative deck artwork across the shelf, selection and matchup
transition without duplicating a giant hero at every step. Crop intentionally,
dim backgrounds under controls, and keep names/format/ownership/source legible.
Existing finish effects and card popup identity remain intact.

## Coin flips are real game actions

- Distinguish a pregame randomizer from a card/ability's coin-flip instruction.
  A coin can be the presentation of a configured coin randomizer; do not rename
  a die roll as a coin or assume every game starts with the same procedure.
- If a call or a keep/ignore decision is required, obtain it at the engine's
  actual choice window. The selected format decides whether a winning randomizer
  grants a play/draw choice; never assume its winner automatically starts.
- The rules authority consumes the correct recorded random stream and commits
  the result once. The coin animation displays that result; visual physics,
  frame timing, repeated clicks, CPU difficulty and browser refresh do not
  decide or reroll it. Hidden future outcomes never enter a CPU observation.
- Retain match/action identity, actor/call when relevant, individual outcomes,
  permitted choices and the final engine result in the accepted trace. Render
  only the viewer-authorized event payload. Multi-coin/replacement/selection
  mechanics require exact card support rather than a generic heads/tails toggle.
- Present a distinct two-sided CardStashy coin with readable face symbols/text,
  material shading, rotation, shadow and a short settle. Show the outcome in
  text and the relevant log entry as well. No requirement depends on sound or
  distinguishing colors alone.
- Skipping or reducing motion reveals the same committed result. Saving before
  a toss, during its presentation or at a subsequent choice restores the correct
  state without a new toss or duplicate effect. A cosmetic interruption cannot
  change a winner, draw, result or history record.

Coin-flip correctness and its animation are separate acceptance items. The
existing Forest/Bears Node smoke test did not exercise coin flips or pregame
play/draw selection and does not close either item.

## Motion and sound with purpose

Cards deal into a fan, lift on inspect, follow a visible source-to-destination
path and settle into their real zone. Tapping has an obvious orientation;
legal/selected/targeted/attacking/blocking states have distinct cues and labels.
Damage, counters, granted abilities and attachment relationships stay readable
after a highlight fades. Preserve printed art while showing effective state.

Mana preview, casting, targets, stack, resolution and triggers must remain
understandable as a sequence. Animate accepted events, cancel stale visual
proposals, and keep authoritative state/controls usable if an asset fails or an
animation is skipped. Prevent replayed events from playing a second action.

Use restrained, separately controllable deal/tap/coin/impact sounds. Respect
mute, reduced motion and saved preferences; verify opening settings during a
decision retains the prompt and returns focus. Never add compulsory screen
shake or long camera transitions. Record render/search/asset costs on tested
desktop hardware rather than promising zero lag.

## Review gates

After the engine proof, compare actual running screens with these references
at representative desktop widths, 100%/200% text scale, both app themes and
reduced motion. Include a full opening hand, crowded hand/battlefield, long
names, multiple attachments, damage plus counters, payment/target choices and
the right rail with a long log. Cards and decisions must remain usable when
background art, texture or optional sound is unavailable.

Retain before/after captures **and short interaction recordings** for deck
selection → matchup → hand → board, inspect/drag/pay/resolve, coin call/toss/
result, and terminal result → deck history. Review native card-art resolution,
crop, shadows, contrast, edge alignment, focus, text clipping and frame timing.
Placeholder art or gradients cannot constitute final visual sign-off.

Coin fixtures must cover both outcomes and seat assignments, applicable calls,
multi-flip/choice effects, recorded-RNG replay, save/restart at pending choices,
skip/reduced motion, repeated clicks and stale/duplicate event delivery. Tests
must check the engine result, event/log, displayed face and restored state agree.
Full-engine tests still need an independent rules oracle and a declared pool.

Visual and functional acceptance are both required before release. No polished
screenshot, recorded animation or default-engine feature label closes a missing
rules, fairness, offline, performance or recovery gate.
